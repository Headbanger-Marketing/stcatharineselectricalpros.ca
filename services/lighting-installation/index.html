Plan the light and the work that makes it possible

A lighting project may involve fixture replacement, new wiring points or a different control layout. The contractor needs to assess existing wiring, mounting support, equipment suitability and access. Fixture appearance alone does not establish compatibility with a dimmer or the suitability of a location. Share product information and the intended room use before installation is priced.

Prepare the scope for your St. Catharines property

Sketch the kitchen in words, marking the counters, cabinet runs and any wall or ceiling that is finished on the other side, and collect the product sheet for each fixture or strip you are considering.

Ask where the new switches or controls will go, whether the under-cabinet lights need their own wiring route, and how the kitchen stays usable on the days the circuit is switched off.

What should the assessment establish?

For each new light in the St. Catharines kitchen, the contractor sets out where its power comes from and how the cable reaches it through cabinets, walls or ceilings, then confirms that each chosen fixture suits its location and any dimmer or control planned. Before the cabinets are installed, any wiring change behind them is named, and the same contractor explains whether the kitchen circuits are shared with other equipment and what that means for using the room during the work. Which surfaces are opened, and who patches and finishes them, are listed in the scope.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can under-cabinet lights be added without opening the kitchen walls?

It depends on where power is available and how the cable can be routed, which only the contractor can judge on site. Ask whether any cable could run inside the cabinets rather than through the wall behind them.

Do my dimmers and new fixtures need to be checked together?

Yes, compatibility should be confirmed before purchase, since fixture appearance does not show it. Send the product sheets to the contractor and ask which control the written scope assumes.
