An extra receptacle does not establish extra capacity

Receptacle locations and circuit design answer different questions. More convenient plug-in points may share the same existing circuit. New appliances or specialty equipment may need a different connection, a dedicated circuit or other assessed work. Give the Licensed Electrical Contractor the equipment information and intended location so the proposal follows the actual use.

Prepare the scope for your St. Catharines property

List what you plan to plug in outside, such as power tools or string lighting, and photograph the wall where each outlet is wanted, noting what finished space lies behind it.

Ask whether the new outlets would share a circuit with indoor rooms, what protection the contractor proposes for outdoor use and how the cable would pass through the wall from the basement or garage.

What should the assessment establish?

Exterior receptacles for tools, lighting or seasonal use are wanted on a St. Catharines house. The contractor traces the circuit each one would use and checks whether it already feeds rooms that must stay in use. Outdoor-rated devices and covers suited to each spot's exposure are specified. If seasonal lighting or a larger tool is planned, a finding follows on whether it needs its own circuit. The route through the wall is described, with any finished surface to be opened and who restores it. The resulting quote is a clear list of locations, equipment and responsibilities.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can an outdoor outlet be added from an indoor circuit in the basement?

Whether an existing circuit can serve it is the contractor's judgment, not something to guess. Ask which circuit is proposed, what else it feeds and what you lose indoors while it is switched off.

What should I tell the contractor about how I will use exterior outlets?

Name each tool, light or device, where it will be used and how often. A string of lights and a power tool place different demands, and the written scope should reflect which one you plan.
