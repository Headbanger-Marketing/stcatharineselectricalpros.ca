EV charger installation in St. Catharines.

Adding a charger to an attached garage in St. Catharines may look simple when the panel is close, but the circuit, the equipment and the supply still need assessing. Short distance does not remove those questions.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your St. Catharines property

Note where the vehicle parks, which wall the charger would mount on and what lies on the other side, such as a finished basement room or a stair. Photograph the garage wall between the charger spot and the panel.

Ask how the cable would travel from the panel to the garage, whether any finished ceiling must be opened and how daily use of the home continues while the circuit is added.

What should the assessment establish?

Either the existing supply can carry a charger on top of everything else the home draws, in which case the report explains how that was judged, or the change it needs is written up as a separate decision. An approved charging unit is named, on a circuit of its own, with outdoor-rated parts wherever it is exposed. The scope names the party that will file the ESA notification and when it is made. The cable route between the attached St. Catharines garage and the panel is described, including any finished surfaces that are opened.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is a charger next to the panel simpler than one across the garage?

Distance is one factor, but the supply, the circuit and the equipment are assessed either way. Ask the contractor to write down what the short run saves and what still needs checking.

What should I tell the contractor about rooms beside the garage wall?

Say what the adjoining rooms are used for and whether the walls are finished. Cable may need to pass through them, and the contractor should explain any opening needed and who restores it.

Useful project details

  • Indoor or outdoor conditions
  • Existing supply records if available
  • Other major loads and future charging needs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
