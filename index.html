Before work begins

Plan the work around the rooms you still need.

Take a bungalow or split-level whose basement is finished or partly finished. In a home like that, the panel may sit inside a finished room or behind storage shelving, and new cable may have to cross finished ceilings and walls.

Whatever the home, list the rooms, appliances and routines that must keep working each day, and photograph the routes work might follow. A contractor can propose an order of work and say when each circuit will be switched off.

Can a room stay in use while its circuit is being worked on?

Sometimes, but the contractor decides, because the supply to a circuit is switched off while it is altered. Ask which rooms lose power on each visit and which circuits can stay in service between visits.

How do I describe a panel that is hidden in a finished basement?

Photograph the closed panel door and the space around it, note what is stored in front and how a person reaches it. Leave any cover closed; the contractor will judge access during the assessment.

How should I compare quotes that sequence the work differently?

Ask each contractor to write the order of work, the rooms affected at each stage and what must be finished before the next stage starts. Quotes with different sequences are not comparable until those points are written down.

Who needs to agree before walls or ceilings are opened?

The owner decides on finishes, and a lender, insurer or landlord may have conditions. Name who can authorize openings in finished areas, and ask the contractor to say who repairs the surfaces afterwards.

Does sending the form start any work in my home?

No. A provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor confirms its identity and licence apart from this site, so look up the ESA licence first, and agrees the job and cost with you, plus the dates.
